# Briar & Bone

**A grim fairytale adventure for your phone, in the spirit of the old top-down Zelda games.**
*Every story has a seam.*

You are the **Reader**. The Briarwood is a storybook that is trying to finish itself, and its Narrator is quietly editing out everything that went wrong. Armed with a Quill, a Book for a shield, a pocket mirror-shard that flips the world between what the story *says* and what *happened*, and a small blot of ink who is (technically) a dog, you walk through fifteen chapters of fairytale country, wake the four seasons, and find out who has been holding the pen.

It is a single self-contained HTML file. No installs, no server, no accounts, no ads, no tracking. It runs offline once loaded and saves to your browser automatically.

> **Play it in landscape.** If you hold your phone upright the game asks you to rotate it.

## The big ideas

### Two worlds
Every room exists as a **Storybook** version (bright, tidy, a little too sure of itself) and a **Truth** version (the same place, with the paint peeled off). Bridges become pits, walls become doors, friendly faces say different things, and some enemies can only be hurt on one side. Most puzzles are really a question about *which world you are standing in.*

### The Four Seasons
The **Wheel of the Year** turns the season in the Briarwood. Spring thaws things and sprouts beans; Summer dries mud and brings the noon; Autumn drops leaves and opens paths; Winter freezes lakes into roads and freezes *you* if you dawdle. Waking each season is a chapter of the story.

### Blot
A small, shy, nervous splash of ink who follows you around and learns tricks as he trusts you: draw ink bridges over torn pages, erase false walls, mark painted-over floor so it holds you, fetch things across gaps, sit on pressure plates, and unleash an Ink Storm. Tricks cost Ink. Forty hidden Inkwells keep him topped up and make him braver.

### Tools, not levels
The old Zelda way: the world is built to be revisited. Chests that look impossible are waiting for a tool you will find later. A dozen tools open the world:

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Quill** | Your sword. Tap to swing, hold to charge a spin attack. Upgradeable |
| **Book** | Your shield. Better books reflect shots |
| **Mirror Shard / Mirror** | Flip between worlds at Seams |
| **Wheel** | Turns the season |
| **Gingerbombs** | Crack weak walls |
| **Braid** | Hooks onto posts and hauls you across gaps |
| **Tinderbox** | Burns thorns and dry grass, lights braziers |
| **Cloak** | Glide over gaps; other cloaks do other things |
| **Mallet** | Smashes rubble |
| **Sickle** | Cuts hedges |
| **Frost Shard** | Freezes water into paths and enemies into targets |
| **Shoes** | A quick dash |
| **White Hart** | A mount, for the places meant for her |
| **Flute** | Songs that open specific places |

Plus gear that needs no button: Eagle Claws, the Selkie Coat, the Lantern, the Hearthstone Mantle.

### The bosses
The Wolf, the Thornmother, the Witch, Gothel, the Shade Warden, the Swan Queen, the Troll, the Harvester, Bluebeard, the Piper, the Long Night, Queen Cinder, the Glass Knight, the Sea Witch, and the Narrator, plus fourteen mini-bosses (the Gingerbread Captain, the Gallery Knight, the Cloak Keeper, the Billy Goat, the Harvest Twins, the Maid of the Keys, the Rat King, the Fairy Godmother, the Eagle Warden, the Drowned Choir, and the three Wardens of the Last Page, among others). Every boss has rules. Learn them.

### The endings
There are **five**. Some are kind. One is a joke. One is a footnote. Reaching one does not close your save: after your first ending, the Last Page waits in the final room so you can choose another (or enter the Boss Rush).

## Things to find

- **Tale Pages (100):** lost pages of the Book. Find them all and something lovely happens.
- **Charms (60):** cosmetic hoods, scarves, boots and trails for the Wardrobe. They change how you look, not how you fight.
- **Inkwells (40):** each one refills Blot, raises his Trust and eventually grows your Ink meter.
- **Burrows (60):** one-room hidden caves behind things that look like scenery. Every one holds a reward.
- **Rings (34):** stone circles. Attune them and you can travel between them. Some want an offering or a puzzle first.
- **Turnstones (13):** records of your progress.
- **Truth Memories:** little remembered moments, only visible in the Truth.
- **Side quests:** the orphans, the brothers, the foreman, the library, and the Lost Chapter trade chain (12 items, many traders).
- **Every chest pays something beyond coins:** a jar, Gingerbombs, a full heal, Ink. Nothing is filler.

### Boss Rush
After your first ending, take on **the Narrator's Greatest Hits**: all 29 fights (15 bosses and 14 mini-bosses) back to back, on a *copy* of your save so nothing in your real game changes. You heal a little between fights, dying ends the run, your best time is kept, and your first clear pays farthings.

## How long is it?

These are estimates, not measurements from real players. They are built from the game's own contents: 262 rooms (202 outside the burrows), roughly 28,000 words of dialogue and text, 318 enemy spawns outside the burrows, roughly 80 rooms with tool-gated puzzles, and 15 + 14 boss fights, using a walking speed of about 5 seconds per room.

| Way of playing | Estimate |
|---|---|
| **Story only**, first playthrough, following the path and picking up what you pass | **5 to 7 hours** (about 5.5 for a typical player) |
| **Story plus a good clean-up** (most Pages, Inkwells, Rings, side quests) | 9 to 11 hours |
| **Everything**: all 60 Burrows, all 100 Pages, 60 Charms, 40 Inkwells, 13 Turnstones, side quests, and all five endings | **12 to 16 hours** |
| **Boss Rush** (29 fights) | 45 to 75 minutes per attempt |

Where the time goes (typical first playthrough, about 5.5 hours): reading and cutscenes about 1.3 h, bosses about 1 h, puzzles and gates about 1.4 h, walking and backtracking about 0.8 h, regular fights about 0.6 h, menus, shops and respawns about 0.4 h.

## Putting it on GitHub Pages

1. Create a repository (for example `briar-and-bone`).
2. Upload `index.html` to the repository root. (You can upload the rest of this folder too.)
3. In the repository go to **Settings, Pages**. Under *Build and deployment* choose **Deploy from a branch**, pick `main` and `/ (root)`, and save.
4. After a minute the game is live at `https://<your-username>.github.io/<repo-name>/`.

Notes:
- The included `.nojekyll` file tells GitHub Pages to serve the file as it is.
- Everything is in the single `index.html`. There is nothing else to host.
- Saves live in each player's own browser (`localStorage`). They do not sync between devices, and clearing site data erases them. In a private/incognito window the game still plays, but progress will not be kept.
- Sound starts after the player's first tap. This is a browser rule, not a bug.

### Adding to a phone home screen
In Safari choose **Share, Add to Home Screen**. In Chrome choose **Add to Home screen**. The game then opens full-screen.

## What is in this folder

```
index.html        The complete game. This is the only file you need to host
README.md         This file
HOW_TO_PLAY.md    The full in-game guide, written out
.nojekyll         Tells GitHub Pages to serve the file as it is
source/           The readable source the game is built from
  build.js          Bundles src/ into one HTML file (run: node build.js)
  src/game.js       Engine, mechanics, bosses, story, UI
  src/data.js       All 262 rooms, puzzles, chests, NPCs and text
  src/gfx.js        Procedural pixel-art drawing (there are no image files)
  src/audio.js      The synthesized music and sound effects
  src/index.template.html   The page shell and styling
  test/             The automated tests used during development
```

### Building from source
```
cd source
node build.js
```
That writes `dist/index.html`. The build needs only Node. The tests under `source/test/` use Playwright and were written against the author's working folder, so file paths inside them need adjusting before they will run elsewhere.

### How it is made
The game draws at a fixed 320 by 180 pixel resolution and scales up crisply. Every sprite, tile and portrait is drawn in code, and every sound and piece of music is synthesized at run time, which is why the whole game is under a megabyte. The rooms are written in a small text-grid language, and a validator checks that every chest, door and boss can actually be reached with the tools the player will have by then, and cannot be reached without them.

## Known limitations (honest list)

- The in-game guide and the playtime figures were written from the game's code and data, not from watching new players. Treat the playtimes as a guide.
- The automated tests play every boss fight and every puzzle room, and a random-input fuzz test has played hundreds of rooms without a crash, but no human has played the whole game from start to finish in one sitting yet. Expect to find a rough edge or two, and please report them.
- Saves are per-browser and per-device. There is no cloud save.
- Landscape only, by design.
- Music begins after the first tap because browsers block autoplaying sound.
